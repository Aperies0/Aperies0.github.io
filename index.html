// featured investigation

A live account takeover, worked end to end

A friend lost about $11,000 in TF2 items to a FACEIT-impersonation phishing operation, followed by a $5,500 crypto ransom demand. I worked it like a SOC case: triage, find the real mechanism, map the attacker's infrastructure, extract IOCs, write the report.

CASE-001 · 2026-07-17 Steam account takeover via FACEIT-impersonation phishing Severity: High Closed · Reported
  1. Alert

    Anomalous session

    Steam flagged a new session from Orem, US (Chrome on Windows), approved through the victim's own mobile authenticator. Items were traded out within the hour.

  2. Triage

    Reconstruct the attack chain

    Nine days of catfish rapport-building, then a Discord call that steered the victim to a fake FACEIT "verification" page.

  3. Intercept

    Test it live

    Walked the phishing flow with a disposable Steam account through Burp Suite and captured the backend requests.

  4. Identify

    Find the real mechanism

    The site pulls a genuine Steam QR login token and shows it as its own. Scanning and approving it hands the attacker a fully authenticated session, no password needed. A second path relays typed credentials to Steam's real login API.

  5. OSINT

    Map attacker infrastructure

    Domain registered 11 days before the attack, behind Cloudflare, with a wildcard cert and IP-based cloaking that serves researchers a 404. A Telegram bot alerts the operator when a target is live. Two level-0 mule accounts held the stolen items.

  6. Report

    Remediate & report

    Session deauthorization, key revocation and password rotation. Abuse reports to the registrar, Cloudflare, Safe Browsing and IC3.
